Cover Letter to DAV Group (BSC)

Carlos Romero Madrid

To: Fernando Cucchietti & Selection Committee
Data Analytics and Visualization Group (DAV)
Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC-CNS)
Application: Scientific Storytelling, Creative Computing & Data Democratization

Dear Fernando and Members of the Data Analytics and Visualization Group,

Most resumes you read probably start with a list of buzzwords. Let me start with a sound: the hum of MareNostrum 5 calculating climate turbulence, or the gentle chime of a motion-sensing sphere when my cat Pipa knocks it across the floor.

I am a Computational Physicist (Double Degree in Physics & Mathematics, 1st of Class MSc in Plasma Physics) and HPC Scientific Software Engineer at BSC. Day in and day out, I optimize petascale solvers across Europe's largest clusters (MN5, Leonardo, LUMI). But here is my honest conviction: if petabytes of scientific breakthroughs stay locked behind cold terminal logs and impenetrable matrices, we have only completed half the mission.

Like the visionary work of Domestic Data Streamers (DDS), I believe data is not just an analytic output—it is raw material waiting to be felt, touched, and inhabited. Science deserves to be democratized, and that requires making it thrilling, tangible, and emotionally resonant.

In my personal creative studio, I bridge rigorous physics with experimental media:

  • MNusic (Listening to & Visualizing MN5 & LUMI): A live synaesthetic engine that turns supercomputing telemetry into real-time harmonic soundscapes (Web Audio API) and generative particle dynamics. Visitors don't just see a load curve; they hear the harmonic pulse of Europe’s computational engine.
  • Pipa-Music-Ball (Bio-Kinetic Physical Computing): An embedded interactive sphere running on an ESP32 and a 6-DoF inertial sensor. When my cat pounces on it, kinetic vectors are converted into microtonal scales. Proving tangible computing can be both scientifically rigorous and playful.
  • Expo-Curator (github.com/carrommad/expo-curator): An open curation platform for automated sub-classification, booklet design, and exhibition wall staging—directly serving the practical spatial needs of galleries and science dissemination centers.
  • Lens & Motion (Photography & Adobe After Effects): Still photographic composition paired with procedural particle animation, kinetic typography, and motion design in After Effects to turn static research figures into broadcast-ready visual essays.

My goal is to bring this exact convergence—rigorous HPC physics and mathematical depth paired with creative visual empathy—directly into the Data Analytics and Visualization Group. Whether designing interactive digital twins (GDU-HPC), communicating urban climate resilience (AIR / REALLOCATE), or staging public scientific installations, I am excited to collaborate on experiences that wow visitors and elevate public understanding of science.

Warm regards,
Carlos Romero Madrid
